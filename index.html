Engineering at the intersection of biology, healthcare, and technology

I am Alvaro Alejandro Cruz Perez, a biomedical engineer and University of Arizona accelerated M.S. candidate. I build practical systems for medical-device development, preclinical imaging, biomechanics, and research, with experience spanning mechanical design, sensors, prototyping, testing, and quantitative analysis.

Approach

I start with the biological, physiological, or clinical need, translate it into engineering requirements, and develop an integrated solution through design, testing, and iteration.

Medical devices Mechanical design Biomechanics Research instrumentation Experimental design

My strongest work comes from combining mechanical design, electronics, programming, biomechanics, imaging, and data analysis instead of treating them as isolated specialties.

Mechanical design

SolidWorks, assemblies, adjustable mechanisms, engineering drawings, 3D printing, and design iteration.

Electromechanical systems

Sensors, actuators, ESP32-based hardware, PCB exposure, soldering, subsystem integration, and troubleshooting.

Biomedical research

Preclinical MRI, experimental design, DeepLabCut, Python, kinematics, MATLAB, and repeatable data-analysis workflows.

Selected Work

Research

Preclinical imaging and movement analysis.

At the University of Arizona Multi-Scale Brain Imaging Lab, I support ferret research involving 7T MRI, behavioral analysis, biomechanics, and custom research equipment. I currently combine technical project ownership with day-to-day coordination of the ferret research team.

SolidWorks3D PrintingPython DeepLabCutMATLABANTs
1

Define requirements

2

Prototype and validate

Iterate in CAD, fabricate components, and test them in the environment where the system will actually be used.

3

Measure and improve

Use imaging, video, and computational workflows to quantify performance, expose failure modes, and guide the next iteration.

About Me

Designing, testing, and learning across disciplines.

I completed my B.S. in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Arizona in May 2026 and am pursuing the accelerated M.S. in Biomedical Engineering, expected May 2027. My interests include medical devices, mechanical design, biomechanics, robotics, research instrumentation, manufacturing, and tissue-engineered technologies.

I am especially drawn to work where I can stay close to the hardware while still using computation and biological understanding to guide design decisions.
