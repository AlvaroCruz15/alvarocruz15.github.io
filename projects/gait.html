Markerless Ferret Gait Analysis

My gait-analysis work expanded my engineering interests from building physical systems to quantitatively understanding how living systems move.

Project snapshot

  • Role: Research Assistant / Project Owner
  • Tools: Python, DeepLabCut
  • Data: multi-camera gait video
  • Focus: kinematics, reliability, longitudinal analysis

This project connected two parts of engineering that I had previously treated separately: the physical motion of a system and the computational tools used to measure and interpret that motion.

The problem

Manual analysis of repeated gait recordings is difficult to scale and can limit quantitative comparison. The goal is to build a reproducible workflow that converts video into useful locomotion and kinematic measures.

My role

I am developing the acquisition, DeepLabCut pose-estimation, filtering, and Python analysis workflow used to quantify movement across animals and timepoints.

What I measure

The workflow extracts measures such as walking speed, step and stride characteristics, joint angles, and angular velocity. I also work on video quality, cropping, confidence thresholds, filtering, smoothing, and validation.

How it shaped my direction

This work deepened my interest in biomechanics, movement, computer vision, and engineering analysis. It has also shown me how software can extend what physical experiments are able to measure.

Growing into leadership

Alongside my own gait and hardware projects, I have taken on broader responsibility within the ferret research team, helping coordinate shared work and routine research operations while supporting teammates working on different projects.
