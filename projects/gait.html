Markerless Ferret Gait Analysis

A developing workflow that uses markerless pose estimation and custom Python analysis to turn multi-camera gait video into reproducible locomotion and kinematic measurements.

Project snapshot

  • Role: Research Assistant / Project Owner
  • Tools: Python, DeepLabCut, OpenCV
  • Acquisition: multi-camera video, up to 60 fps
  • Focus: kinematics, repeatability, longitudinal analysis
This page describes the workflow at a high level. Unpublished animal data, raw videos, and internal analysis results are not posted.

This work connected two parts of engineering that I had previously treated separately: the mechanics of movement and the computational tools used to measure, filter, and interpret that movement.

The problem

Gait analysis is difficult to scale across repeated subjects, and longitudinal timepoints without specialized equipment. The goal is to build a reproducible pipeline that converts video into interpretable measures while making the uncertainty and quality-control steps explicit.

My role

As the technical owner of the project, I am responsible for the video-acquisition, DeepLabCut pose-estimation, filtering, and Python-analysis workflow. My role is to define and refine the experimental and analytical pipeline, including camera setup, video cropping, confidence thresholds, smoothing, interpolation, and quality-control procedures to improve measurement reliability across recording conditions. At the same time, as the leader of the ferret research team, I help maintain consistency across related research activities, including MRI acquisitions and other behavioral data collection, so that gait measurements can be integrated with the broader study.

Metrics under development

The analysis extracts walking speed, step and stride characteristics, step and stride length, hip-height normalization, joint-angle measures, and angular velocity. The workflow keeps clips and subjects traceable so metrics can be compared across repeated timepoints.

Why validation matters

Pose estimation is only useful when confidence, visibility, frame rate, filtering, and event detection are handled consistently. I treat model training and post-processing as part of the measurement system rather than assuming that predicted keypoints are automatically correct.

Related machine-learning research

A parallel ferret-behavior project uses DeepLabCut as part of an automated burrowing-annotation pipeline. That work has strengthened my experience with training-set design, architecture comparison, error evaluation, and scaling video analysis across many recordings.
