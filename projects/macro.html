Capstone • Mechanical Lead • Electromechanical Design

MACRO (Multi-digit Assessment of Choice Response Output) Device

My capstone project was the point where I took full ownership of the mechanical design within a multidisciplinary device team, translating user and system needs into adjustable physical hardware that had to work with sensing, actuation, electronics, and software.

Project snapshot

  • Role: Mechanical Engineering Lead
  • Tools: SolidWorks, 3D printing
  • System interfaces: FSRs, LRAs, ESP32-based electronics, Python GUI
  • Focus: adjustability, ergonomics, repeatability, serviceability
NDA: only general responsibilities, engineering process, and non-confidential lessons are shown. Restricted images, schematics, test data, sponsor materials, and protected design details are intentionally omitted.

This project taught me that mechanical design is not isolated CAD work. Geometry affects sensors, electronics, wiring, software, user setup, assembly, and the reliability of the entire system.

The system problem

The device required repeatable positioning of a user's hand and individual digits while supporting tactile stimulation and force sensing. The mechanical architecture needed to accommodate different hand sizes while keeping setup consistent and maintaining clean interfaces with the electrical and software subsystems.

My role

As Mechanical Engineering Lead, I owned development of adjustable hand-positioning components and supporting structures in SolidWorks. I coordinated mechanical interfaces with sensors, actuators, wiring, embedded electronics, and the operating workflow used by the team.

How I approached the design

I iterated 3D-printed components around ergonomics, adjustability, compact packaging, assembly, accessible wiring, serviceability, and standardized subject setup. Each revision forced tradeoffs between ideal geometry and what could be fabricated, assembled, maintained, and used reliably.

Integration lessons

The most important lesson was to design mechanical packaging and electrical access together. A component can fit in CAD yet still create problems if wires cannot be routed, sensors cannot be serviced, or the system requires unnecessary disassembly to troubleshoot.
