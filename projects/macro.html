MACRO Hand Assessment Device

My capstone project marked the point where mechanical design became a central part of my engineering identity. I was responsible for turning system and user requirements into adjustable physical hardware.

Project snapshot

  • Role: Mechanical Engineering Lead
  • Tools: SolidWorks, 3D printing
  • Interfaces: FSRs, LRAs, embedded electronics
  • Focus: adjustability, ergonomics, repeatability

This project taught me that mechanical design is not isolated CAD work. Geometry affects sensors, electronics, software, user setup, fabrication, and the reliability of the entire system.

The problem

The device required repeatable positioning of a user's hand and individual digits while supporting tactile stimulation and response sensing. The mechanical system had to work across different hand sizes and integrate cleanly with the electrical and software subsystems.

My role

As Mechanical Engineering Lead, I developed and refined the hand block, finger-positioning rails, adjustable features, and supporting mechanical components in SolidWorks while coordinating mechanical decisions with the rest of the capstone team.

The design process

I iterated 3D-printed components around fit, adjustability, compact packaging, assembly, and standardized subject setup. Each iteration forced tradeoffs between ideal geometry and what was practical to fabricate, assemble, and use.

How it shaped my direction

MACRO strengthened my interest in mechanical design, human-centered hardware, biomechanics, and electromechanical systems. It also gave me confidence taking ownership of a subsystem while still designing as part of a larger engineering team.
