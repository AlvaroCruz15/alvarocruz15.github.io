MRI-Compatible Ferret Cradle

This project moved me from class-based design into research equipment that had to function inside a real experimental workflow and a highly constrained MRI environment.

Project snapshot

  • Role: Research Assistant / Designer
  • Tools: SolidWorks, rapid prototyping
  • Environment: 7T Bruker MRI
  • Focus: compatibility, fit, usability, repeatability
Only publish images, drawings, code, data, or technical details that are cleared for public release.

The cradle taught me that a successful design is defined by its environment. A part can be mechanically sound and still fail if it interferes with imaging, workflow, positioning, safety, or repeatability.

The problem

The research team needed a stable, repeatable, MRI-compatible way to position ferrets for imaging while working within tight scanner and coil geometry and the practical needs of veterinary and imaging personnel.

My role

I translated user and experimental needs into SolidWorks designs, developed a modular rail-and-hook attachment concept, built prototypes, and tested iterations in the MRI environment.

Design under constraints

The work required balancing geometry, material compatibility, animal safety, positioning, attachment, usability, and imaging constraints rather than optimizing only one parameter.

How it shaped my direction

This project made the design-build-test cycle central to how I approach engineering. It also increased my interest in research instrumentation and custom hardware that enables scientific work.
