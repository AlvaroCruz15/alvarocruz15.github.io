Heat Illness Detection Wearable

One of my earlier device projects, where I learned that integrating sensors and electronics is often less about connecting blocks on a diagram and more about systematically finding what is failing between them.

Project snapshot

  • Focus: wearable biomedical sensing
  • Hardware: pulse oximeter, accelerometer, thermistors
  • Skills: soldering, PCB work, troubleshooting
  • Role in my development: system integration

This project changed the way I troubleshoot. Instead of treating a device as one object, I learned to isolate subsystems, verify interfaces, and build confidence one layer at a time.

The problem

The goal was to develop a wearable system combining physiological, motion, and temperature sensing for heat-illness-related monitoring. The project required multiple sensors and embedded hardware to function together reliably.

What I worked on

I contributed to integrating the pulse-oximetry, accelerometer, and temperature-sensing hardware and worked through communication and circuit problems when the prototype did not behave as expected.

What I learned

I used voltage checks, component isolation, trace review, circuit rework, and subsystem testing to identify problems. The device was not a perfect final product, but the troubleshooting process became one of the most valuable parts of the project.

How it shaped my direction

This was an early step toward my interest in hands-on engineering. It made me want to work closer to hardware, where design decisions, interfaces, fabrication, and debugging have visible consequences.
